# Regulatory impact

Read the [landscape](../../../docs/research/regulatory-landscape.md), [compliance matrix](../../../docs/requirements/compliance-matrix.md) and the affected source entries. Confirm the laboratory's jurisdiction (and, for hosted services, the service provider's), testing program, specimen/method, data provenance and intended use; record unknowns without inventing answers.

For each claim, distinguish statute/regulation, final-but-not-effective rule, proposal, accreditation condition, contract/payer term and project recommendation. Check the underlying authority and current effective status. Do not reuse obsolete LDT phaseout dates or describe proposed HIPAA/CLIA changes as enacted.

Explain what changes in the product, operating procedure, configuration, evidence and human ownership. Add or update stable REG identifiers with applicability and a direct source. Preserve distinctions between clinical records, compliance documentation, specimens and audit retention. Part 2, Part 11, DOT and forensic rules are conditional analyses, not universal LIS labels. Payer coverage policies (MAC LCDs and billing articles), fraud-and-abuse laws (AKS, Stark, EKRA), information blocking, Section 504/1557 accessibility and FDA software-function boundaries also shape the design. Record payer conditions as varying by contractor, payer and date of service; leave arrangement legality to counsel.

Identify what public sources cannot establish, including licensed accreditation clauses, state-specific scope and contractual service coverage. Route those to the responsible owner with a concrete evidence request. Return an impact assessment and review needs; do not certify compliance or make clinical/legal approvals on behalf of the lab.
