# Toxicology laboratory practices and design consequences

Status: practice evidence checked 2026-10-12 for US clinical toxicology, including [outpatient drug-monitoring practices](#outpatient-drug-monitoring-practices). Urine, blood, oral fluid and other matrices need their own method and collection configuration; none is selected here ([working assumptions](../project-scope.md#remaining-working-assumptions)). Workplace, forensic and postmortem rules are outside scope and are not clinical configuration defaults.

Source IDs resolve in [the domain source register](sources-domain.md). Workflow and software recommendations below are project inferences; the laboratory director must supply and approve actual assay rules. This document intentionally defines no drug cutoff values or patient-specific interpretations.

## Practices that shape the model

ARUP distinguishes method certainty from output scale: immunoassay results are presumptive, mass-spectrometry testing is generally definitive, and either workflow can produce qualitative or quantitative outputs. A definitive test may be ordered first or follow a screen. Urine concentration alone does not establish an administered dose. These distinctions must survive ordering, review and reporting. [DOM-001](https://arupconsult.com/content/drug-testing)

| Concept | Proposed representation and consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Clinical purpose | Capture ordering context and authorized test request without diagnosing misuse or nonadherence from a result. Any interpretation is authored/approved under laboratory policy. |
| Panel versus analyte | An orderable panel version expands to explicit requested tests; each test may report several analytes/metabolites. Preserve originally requested components, conditional reflex components and their terminal dispositions when the catalog changes. Measured analytes outside the authorized report scope do not become reportable merely because they appear in an export. |
| Sequence versus certainty | Screening, confirmation and first-line testing describe clinical sequence. Presumptive/definitive describe method certainty; qualitative/quantitative describe output scale. Do not infer one property solely from another. |
| Drug class versus individual analyte | Keep distinct identifiers. A class screen must not be rewritten as a molecule-specific confirmation. |
| Parent drug and metabolite | Model distinct measured analytes plus curated, versioned relationships. Relationships support display and specialist review; they are not automatic proof of ingestion or a prescribed dose. |
| Method versus instrument | A validated method version identifies its matrix, preparation, instrument/software compatibility, reportable analytes and rules. An instrument serial number alone is insufficient provenance. |
| Testing program | Store clinical/workplace/forensic purpose explicitly; use program-specific authorization, custody, report and retention policies. |

Mayo's specimen-validity service measures several characteristics, including creatinine, specific gravity, pH and oxidants, with algorithmic follow-up and classifications. Its published thresholds belong to its assay and are not adopted here. [DOM-002](https://www.mayocliniclabs.com/test-catalog/Overview/29345), [DOM-003](https://www.mayocliniclabs.com/-/media/it-mmfiles/Special-Instructions/2/8/9/Adulterant_Survey_Algorithm.pdf)

Proposed consequence: specimen-validity observations, review decisions and classifications are separate from drug results and from a collection/transport rejection. A drug-negative outcome must never erase an invalid or unresolved specimen-validity condition. Validity checks and resulting holds are defined by the approved method/program; no universal rule is embedded in the LIS. Clinical drug-monitoring consequences are [below](#specimen-validity-and-other-matrices).

SAMHSA describes primary and split specimens and MRO-related processes in federal workplace programs. These are useful future design cases, but clinical internal specimen tracking does not establish regulated chain-of-custody compliance. [DOM-004](https://www.samhsa.gov/substance-use/drug-free-workplace/drug-testing-resources), [DOM-005](https://www.samhsa.gov/sites/default/files/urine-collection-handbook-2024.pdf)

## Proposed preanalytical controls

1. Record the patient/donor identity asserted by each source, collection event, requestor, collection site, specimen matrix, container, collector and relevant transport conditions. Preserve unknown/estimated times explicitly instead of inventing exact timestamps.
2. Separate order, accession, biological specimen, physical container and prepared aliquot identifiers. A recollection is a new specimen linked to the reason and original order; it is not a relabeled original specimen.
3. At receipt, record label agreement, quantity, container integrity, required seals, collection metadata and rejection/conditional-acceptance reasons against the applicable SOP version. Hold identity uncertainty rather than selecting a plausible patient automatically.
4. Barcode movements and lineage. Aliquoting, transfers, dilution and extraction are recorded operations with actor, time, method and parent material. Reserve volumes and split-container restrictions are enforceable.
5. Preserve collection-to-receipt and storage histories; stability windows are matrix/method-specific configuration, not a global number. Quarantine an excursion pending authorized assessment.

## Proposed analytical controls

Preparation batches and instrument runs are different objects: one prep batch may feed several runs and one run may combine several preparations. Every run position resolves to patient material, a control, a calibrator or a blank; no unresolved sample ID becomes a patient result. Carry forward reagent, control, calibrator and consumable lot identifiers needed by the local method.

Freeze the applicable acquisition/processing method, calibration evidence, QC policy, analyte mapping, units and reporting rules when each test execution is authorized. A newer configuration never silently reinterprets historical results. An authorized reprocessing event creates a new evaluation with its own software/method version and reason while retaining the original output.

An analytical repeat, dilution or re-extraction under the original request differs from ordering a new test or triggering a reflex. Store the order authority, requested scope and reason separately from technical execution history and payment decisions. Add-ons need a new suitability assessment of remaining material, including matrix, stability, preparation compatibility, reservations and holds. These are proposed safeguards, not a claim that every repeat needs another clinician order.

Record analytical evidence supplied by supported vendor software, such as integration flags, internal-standard evidence, calibration status and QC decisions. Which evidence is mandatory is method-specific. The LIS coordinates review and traceability; initial scope does not reproduce proprietary chromatographic acquisition or peak integration. Official SCIEX and Waters guidance shows result export routes with product/version/template dependencies. [DOM-020](https://sciex.com/support/knowledge-base-articles/how-to-interface-multiquant-with-lims-system-via-lims-report-template_en_us), [DOM-021](https://help.waters.com/content/dam/waters/ko/support/usermanuals/2024/715008798/715008798.pdf) Where vendor quantitation software or a hosted review platform certifies results before export, the LIS records that certification, its human or rule-based nature and each re-export as evidence; it is not LIS technical review unless laboratory policy maps a named authorized human certification ([domain model](../architecture/domain-model.md#upstream-analytical-review-and-re-export), Q-25).

QC failures block affected release according to the validated policy and applicable QC obligations. The system must retain failed observations and excluded calibration points. Rerun, recalibration or a justified change in affected scope preserves the failed evidence; a local exception cannot waive required QC acceptance. Identity mismatch, missing provenance and failed authentication are nonoverridable release gates. Link each result to preparation, calibration, run, analyte and sample-level evidence: passed run QC cannot cure a failed extraction shared by several runs. A later-discovered analytical defect opens an impact assessment for already issued reports. The whole analytical process and reporting acceptance are addressed in [CLIA 493.1256](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-G/part-493/subpart-K/subject-group-ECFRc96daead380f6ed/section-493.1256); exact method controls remain laboratory decisions ([SR-006](sources-regulatory.md)).

## Proposed result semantics

| Item | Required distinction |
| --- | --- |
| Original measurement | Preserve source value/text, reported units, source precision, flags, file checksum and processing revision. It is evidence, not automatically a releasable observation. |
| Exact numeric result | Decimal magnitude, unit code, display precision and rule version; avoid binary floating-point for authoritative concentrations and threshold comparisons. |
| Censored result | Store comparator and bound separately: below a reporting limit, above an upper limit or outside an accepted range. Never coerce to zero or to the bound as an exact measurement. |
| Qualitative result | Approved coded term and display text, with method certainty. Detected, not detected, presumptive positive, indeterminate, invalid and not performed must not collapse into a boolean. |
| Missing result | Explicit reason such as insufficient volume, interference, cancelled, pending or instrument error. Absence of a value is not a negative result. |
| Cutoff | A versioned decision threshold with units, comparison operator, scope, rounding rule and effective date. It is distinct from detection/quantitation limits and a population reference interval. |
| Reporting limits | Lower/upper reportable limits, analytical measurement interval, dilution validation and censoring behavior are explicit method configuration. No global handling across assays. |
| Reference interval | Store separately when applicable with population/method context. Do not fabricate a normal range for a drug-detection assay. |
| Rounding | Preserve the source measurement. Apply the approved comparison/rounding order and display precision; test both sides of a decision boundary. |
| Derived quantity | Retain inputs, formula version, units and authorization. A normalization or ratio cannot overwrite its source observations. |

Waters documents differences between summary-table decimal formatting and LIMS/XML export behavior. Proposed adapter acceptance therefore includes known decimal-boundary examples and comparison with the vendor's reviewed results, rather than assuming all exports preserve equal precision. [DOM-023](https://support.waters.com/KB_Inf/MassLynx/WKB56380_Can_I_change_the_decimal_places_that_are_exported_from_TargetLynx)

## Proposed postanalytical controls

Technical review assesses specimen, analytical and result evidence and binds approval to the selected analyte/result revisions and their dependencies; authorized release records a distinct accountable decision under the lab's privileges and SOP. Selecting a result from another attempt or changing its evidence invalidates affected approval. Two distinct people are required only where the approved policy requires separation, but a single click must not hide the two decisions. Autoverification is deferred until the exact rules, edge cases and failure modes have been validated.

Reports reference immutable result revisions and include relevant method/cutoff context, limitations, specimen/collection details, performing lab and approved interpretation. Completeness and amendment status are independent: a corrected partial report still has outstanding components. A correction creates an attributable new report revision and recipient-specific notification obligations. Wrong-patient corrections require distinct withdrawal/error notices and authorized disclosure of corrected content. Cancelled tests and late referral results remain visible; a report already transmitted is never rewritten in place.

Referral reconciliation must preserve the testing laboratory's results and related interpretation, identify its location and retain an exact report copy even when the testing laboratory delivers directly. Local formatting must not change that meaning. Reporting delays also need a documented urgency-based notification decision, beyond a queue-age metric. These duties are supported by [CLIA 493.1291(h)–(i)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-G/part-493/subpart-K/subject-group-ECFR9482366886d579f/section-493.1291) ([SR-008](sources-regulatory.md)); local workflow details require approval.

Billing receives an explicit performed-service event under an approved billing policy. Clinical indication, reflex authorization, test performance and billable coding are separate facts. Repeating an interface message or instrument run must not duplicate a charge. Full revenue-cycle management, payer-specific logic and reimbursement advice are outside the initial pilot; the [class-based service derivation](#class-based-service-derivation) needed at the handoff boundary is not.

Storage disposal and digital retention use separate, configurable policies with legal holds, affected-record inventory, authorization and audit evidence. Downtime requires controlled local records and later reconciliation of identifiers, provenance, reports and duplicate submissions; it does not justify unaudited backdating.

## Outpatient drug-monitoring practices

The target is physician-office and small clinical toxicology laboratories that fit one computer, including outpatient pain-management and substance-use-disorder (SUD) treatment workflows (Q-20, Q-33). Sources are registered in the [drug-monitoring ledger section](sources-domain.md#outpatient-drug-monitoring-payer-documentation-and-emerging-substances). They describe how clinicians and payers use laboratory work; they supply no ordering, coverage, class or interpretation rules. Software enforces only rules that the laboratory or its billing/compliance owner supplies and approves, and never decides medical necessity.

### Individualized ordering and order evidence

Guidance ties drug testing to a patient-specific clinical question. CDC asks prescribers to weigh benefits and risks, know which drugs a panel includes, reserve confirmation for results that can change management and avoid punitive use (DOM-027). The ADLM (then AACC) pain-management guideline prefers qualitative definitive testing for adherence and describes tiered scopes rather than one universal panel (DOM-024). ASAM's 2017 consensus remains the published document; a joint ASAM/ACMT **draft** (public comment May/June 2026) recommends individualized testing decisions and no universal screening testing (DOM-026).

Medicare places documentation duties on the testing entity: keep what the orderer supplied and proof that the claim reflects it, and produce the order, processing evidence and supplied diagnostic information on request (SR-038; DOM-035, DOM-036). One MAC's LCD requires written individualized orders listing each drug or class, a legible signature and documented rationale; it distinguishes patient-specific standing orders (limited sequential visits, alterable by the clinician) and patient-specific profiles from blanket or routine standing orders for every patient in a practice, which it does not cover; it covers reference-laboratory reflex but not reflex after point-of-care presumptive testing, and covers direct-to-definitive testing (SR-035). Its billing article, whose current status needs confirmation, tells laboratories not to prepopulate requisitions with diagnosis codes (DOM-032). Other contractors require drugs/classes on written orders and the orderer's NPI on claims (DOM-033, DOM-034). CMS discourages check-box templates as necessity evidence and applies signature rules from the governing statute, regulation, NCD or LCD (DOM-036). These are contractor- and date-specific examples; commercial and Medicaid policies differ (REG-002, REG-025).

Proposed consequences:

- The order records the clinician's per-patient choice of method certainty and drug classes/analytes, the ordering practitioner (with NPI and, when billing Medicare, a dated enrollment check), signature or intent-to-order evidence, any standing order or protocol copy with its scope and expiry, diagnosis codes or narrative **as supplied**, billing responsibility, collection date and any client point-of-care presumptive result as attributed clinical context, never as a laboratory observation.
- Completeness is checked against versioned laboratory/payer order policies (required elements, standing-order validity, prohibited practice-wide profiles). Missing order authority, including a signature/intent that laboratory policy requires, holds the order before testing; missing payer documentation is a billing review item, never a clinical hold (REG-029, REG-030). No screen or export asserts medical necessity.
- Diagnosis codes and indications are never defaulted, inferred or auto-filled. A practice order set is a template; where policy requires individualization, the per-patient selection is recorded explicitly, and a practice-wide identical profile is never auto-applied but flagged for compliance review.

### Billing intake ownership

Reusable patient coverage and guarantor information should be confirmed for each order, with the selected version preserved through the billing handoff. Accessioning reconciles the requisition and collection facts against that order context. Quest documents patient-level reuse with order-time editing; HL7 distinguishes patient, subscriber, guarantor and selected coverage (DOM-175–179). These are design evidence, not a mandatory schema. The [billing evidence and handoff guide](../operations/billing-evidence-and-handoff.md) describes patient financial profiles, immutable order-specific selections, manual evidence and correction handling in the current implementation. Q-16/Q-21 must settle the external billing boundary, completeness/correction workflow and service-specific notice evidence. Missing financial information remains billing work, separate from clinical authority and specimen/identity holds.

### Class-based service derivation

Medicare definitive drug-testing HCPCS codes are per-day services tiered by the number of drug classes tested, including metabolites, with a separate definitive code for methods lacking the descriptor's calibration/internal-standard/QC elements; presumptive CPT codes differ by method complexity. One code per family is reported per date of service, and urine validity testing is not separately billed (DOM-029, DOM-030). The Medicare date of service is generally the collection date (DOM-031). OIG found no consistent contractor guidance on counting classes, and six of seven sampled laboratories counted them differently; a later audit linked routine highest-tier billing to at-risk providers (DOM-038, DOM-039). The usual class taxonomy is in the licensed CPT code set (DOM-015).

Proposed consequences: an analyte joins a billing class only through a versioned, approved mapping that names its source and effective dates. Derivation groups by patient and configured date-of-service rule across accessions, counts classes executed within the ordered scope and is independent of result positivity. Technical repeats never add classes; cancelled or never-performed components are excluded; performed-not-ordered items are excluded and flagged, with separate clinical follow-up; a failed attempt counts only under the billing owner's approved policy, within the ordered scope and never in addition to a successful repeat (Q-21). Report display grouping is separate configuration. The billing owner approves code assignment; the LIS provides an explainable derivation, not reimbursement advice, and ships no CPT content.

### Medication-consistency interpretation

Medication-consistency reporting, when offered by the laboratory, compares findings with the supplied medication list. The comparison is not a lookup: metabolic pathways, minor metabolites near reporting limits, pharmaceutical impurities and genetic variability can produce or remove expected findings, and urine concentration does not establish dose or adherence (DOM-001, DOM-024). Professional guidance frames unexpected results as prompts for nonjudgmental clinical discussion and confirmation where consequential (DOM-026, DOM-027).

Proposed consequences:

- **Medication statement:** text as received, mapped identity under a versioned medication mapping, source (clinician list, patient report relayed by the clinician or other), reporter and provided date. Unmapped entries remain visible and receive no automatic comparison.
- **Rule content:** laboratory-authored, approved and versioned per method/matrix, expressing expected parent/metabolite analytes. Outputs are limited to deterministic comparison categories (for example expected-detected, expected-not-detected, unexpected-detected, not tested) with laboratory-approved labels and no dose, timing, adherence or diversion conclusion. The project ships no relationship content; the capability stays disabled until the laboratory approves and validates it and the intended-use assessment is recorded (Q-22, Q-26; REG-024, REG-040).
- **Annotation:** derived from frozen inputs (selected result revisions, medication snapshot, rule version, validity state), shown separately from analytical results, never altering them, and released under laboratory authority. An expected analyte outside the executed scope is *not tested*, never *not detected*; presumptive-only or validity-compromised inputs are labeled or held under policy. The report shows the medication list's as-of date; a list older than the laboratory's maximum age is withheld from comparison or flagged under policy. Any post-release change to released annotation or medication-list content creates a new report revision.

### Specimen validity and other matrices

The ADLM guideline recommends validity testing on pain-management urine drug tests, with point-of-collection temperature/pH and laboratory creatinine and oxidant testing, and no further testing of specimens outside acceptance criteria (DOM-024). Dilution allows false negatives (DOM-001). CLIA reports must state the condition and disposition of specimens not meeting acceptability criteria (SR-008). Federal workplace dilute/substituted/adulterated/invalid definitions are program-specific, not clinical defaults (DOM-004). Proposed consequence: laboratory-defined validity categories per matrix map to approved report actions (hold, qualifier on negative findings, cancellation with recollection request or comment); collection-site observations are collector-attributed collection data, distinct from laboratory measurements; validity work generates no separate service unless billing policy says so.

Urine is the preferred matrix in the pain-management guideline; serum/plasma suits anuric patients and oral fluid suits selected drugs (DOM-024). Oral-fluid windows approximate blood, not all drugs are detectable, and false negatives exceed urine (DOM-001, DOM-025); the ASAM draft suggests oral fluid when observed urine collection is inappropriate (DOM-026). Each matrix therefore needs its own methods, limits, validity concepts, devices and stability. Federal workplace oral-fluid rules remain separate (REG-021).

### Menu change and emerging substances

ADLM guidance says menus should mirror local drug-use patterns, include synthetic opioids such as fentanyl where assays exist, may need specialized mass spectrometry for nitazenes, and notes rapidly changing xylazine prevalence (DOM-025); the pain guideline describes adding locally common and ever-changing synthetic compounds (DOM-024). Targeted mass spectrometry reports only specified analytes and opiate immunoassays miss fully synthetic opioids (DOM-001). CDC records ONDCP's emerging-threat declaration for xylazine-adulterated fentanyl (DOM-028). Medetomidine and current trend reports remain unverified in this evidence baseline.

Proposed consequences: adding an analyte, class or matrix is a method change with validation evidence (REG-004) and must carry its terminology mapping, limits, report placement, billing-class mapping and interpretive-rule impact before activation. In-flight orders keep frozen scope; reports state what was tested; reprocessing archived data or retained material for a new analyte needs order authority. Frequent change makes configuration comparison, effective dating and impact review core functions.

### Reports, patient access and payer records

Cutoffs should be defined and easily accessible, immunoassay phrasing should reflect false-positive potential, shared presumptive results should be labeled, and laboratories should supply concise interpretive reports and consultation; the pain guideline also asks that non-FDA-approved tests be noted (DOM-024, DOM-025, DOM-026). Because clinicians must know a panel's contents (DOM-027), the proposed drug-monitoring report lists every tested analyte including not-detected ones, method certainty, cutoff/limits, validity outcome, medication list with source/date, interpretive comments with rule version and approver, and explicit not-tested entries, alongside required CLIA elements (SR-008). Disclaimer wording belongs to the regulatory owner (REG-024).

CLIA permits laboratories to give patients or representatives completed reports on request using the laboratory's authentication process (SR-008); HIPAA access applies where the laboratory is a covered entity (SR-036). The patient receives the issued revision with current status, subject to Part 2 determinations (REG-018/019) and state direct-access limits (REG-042). Laboratories are information-blocking actors: complete electronic export of designated-record-set content is needed, and delays must be individualized or patient-requested rather than configured blanket embargoes (REG-036). Patient- and client-facing digital content may need WCAG 2.1 AA conformance where Section 504 applies (REG-039).

Medicare reviewers request from the billing laboratory the order with orderer contact details, evidence of accurate processing and claim submission, and supplied diagnostic information, usually within 45 days or 30 days for UPICs (DOM-036, DOM-037). Proposed consequence: assemble an access-controlled, minimum-necessary evidence package from retained records with deadline and disclosure tracking. The clinician's medical record remains the orderer's; the laboratory neither fabricates nor edits evidence to answer a request.

## Portal ordering and delivery evidence

A future client portal and secure report delivery are proposed optional services outside current implementation scope ([scope](../project-scope.md#product-boundaries), [external-service evidence](cloud-services.md#41-client-ordering-portal)). The conditional requirements REQ-ORD-008–011 and REQ-RPT-011–014 in the [functional requirements](../requirements/functional-requirements.md) extend the order-evidence practices above (REQ-ORD-005/006/007) and add no clinical authority. Their practice evidence:

- **No prepopulation (REQ-ORD-010).** The contractor warning against prepopulated requisitions (DOM-032) and CMS's discouragement of check-box templates as necessity evidence (DOM-036) support recording only what the clinician selects for this patient. Payer-documentation gaps are billing review items, not clinical holds (REG-029/030).
- **Self-sufficient requisition (REQ-ORD-009).** A specimen can reach the lab before an order submitted while the lab computer is off. 42 CFR 493.1241 fixes minimum requisition content whatever a vendor's form omits (DOM-052).
- **Receipt states (REQ-ORD-008).** ONC's SAFER guide expects tracking through collected, received, completed, reported and acknowledged (DOM-051). A portal order is a request until the LIS accepts it.
- **Limited-use boundary (REQ-ORD-011).** Scheduling, e-prescribing, charting and other labs' results stay outside a laboratory-furnished portal for fraud-and-abuse reasons (Q-32, [regulatory §15.5](regulatory-landscape.md#155-fraud-and-abuse-laboratory-furnished-clinic-portals)).
- **Delivery evidence by channel (REQ-RPT-011–014, INV-15).** Each channel's available signal and its limits are in [external-service evidence §6](cloud-services.md#6-delivery-channel-confirmation) (DOM-067 to DOM-078). CLIA requires the laboratory to retain the report copy and to notify when a correction changes a released result (SR-008), so per-destination delivery evidence is part of the laboratory's own record. [Regulatory §15.4](regulatory-landscape.md#154-clia-with-one-pc-as-the-laboratorys-record-system) covers periodic intact-arrival checks (REG-047).

## Decisions requiring laboratory evidence

The first design partner must approve the test menu, matrices, methods, authorized reflexes, validity policy, review privileges, analytical exceptions, report wording, actual cutoffs/reporting limits, retention schedules, critical/urgent notification policy and downtime SOP. For drug monitoring it must also supply the payer/order-completeness policies, drug-class source and analyte mapping, date-of-service rule, medication terminology, any medication-consistency rule content and vocabulary, validity report consequences per matrix, menu-change governance, retained-specimen retest/dispute policy and patient-access channel. These map to open questions Q-21 (payer and order/billing policy), Q-22 (medication consistency), Q-23 (menu change), Q-24 (validity and retained specimens), Q-25 (upstream review layer), Q-26 (regulated-function boundary) and Q-27 (compliance program and client arrangements) in the [decision queue](../planning/open-questions.md). Until then, these are requirements for configurable behavior, not operating instructions for a laboratory.
